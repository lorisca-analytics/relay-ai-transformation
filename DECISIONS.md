# Decisions

Each entry: the options, the one I picked, why, and what it cost. The first five are calls I made in the case. The last four are choices in the rebuild.

## 1. What actually broke in December

- **Options:** a forecasting gap, an operations failure fixed with better machinery, a coordination failure.
- **Picked:** coordination.
- **Why:** every function did its own job. The signal existed in three places and none of them had an owner or a route to someone who could act.
- **Given up:** the founder's simpler reading that December was an operations failure to fix with better machinery.

## 2. The $2.8M personalization engine

- **Options:** sign now and negotiate later, pilot one module, turn it down and fund nothing yet, turn it down and fund the internal alarm.
- **Picked:** turn it down and fund the alarm.
- **Why:** the contract let the vendor use customer data to improve its models for its other clients, which could include rivals. Its promised gains were clicks and opens, with nothing on margin or returns. And it would add more demand push on top of the missing alarm that caused December.
- **Given up:** a visible "AI launch" the board would have liked, and months of speed if the vendor's claims were real.

## 3. The first 48 hours after the voice-cloning incident

- **Options:** apologise and pause all AI, go public now, fix the system first and speak later, repair in private with the ambassador and the community first, then speak.
- **Picked:** private repair first, public statement after, together with them.
- **Why:** going public first would repeat the original mistake of speaking for the community without it in the room.
- **Given up:** control of the story for a few days. The CMO wanted to move faster.

## 4. Which three measures go on the panel

- **Options:** nine candidates, including days of stock cover, long-tenured customer retention, an AI oversight gap, and the return gap between standard and plus sizes.
- **Picked:** customer value by channel (returns counted), ambassador retention and trust, and how fast demand signals reach action.
- **Why:** each one names a decision, an owner and a signal speed, and each comes with a guard-rail so it can't improve while the company gets worse.
- **Given up:** the return gap between standard and plus sizes, which I'd first ranked in the top three. It still drives the sizing check here, as the evidence behind decision 5.

## 5. The founder's "no AI touches a customer" rule

- **Options:** accept the ban, push back on the community-as-vault idea, push back on the ban.
- **Picked:** push back on the ban.
- **Why:** AI already touched customers through the sizing tool, and that tool was worse for plus sizes with no owner. A ban hides the risk. An owner, a bias check and a human override fix it.
- **Given up:** the easy agreement of telling the founder what she wanted.

## 6. Rebuild the prototype or polish the first version

- **Options:** fix the first version, rebuild from scratch, rebuild while keeping its structure.
- **Picked:** rebuild, keeping the four parts and their order.
- **Why:** the first version showed numbers that changed at random to look live, had no clock or escalation on alerts, and carried a claim the data didn't support. Fixing that meant rewriting most of the logic anyway.
- **Given up:** the first version's exact visual design. This version uses my portfolio's look.

## 7. AI check or rule check for messages

- **Options:** call a language model to judge each message, or use plain rules plus a required human approval.
- **Picked:** plain rules plus approval.
- **Why:** a public static page can't hold an API key safely, and a rule check is honest about what it catches. The approval step is the real safeguard either way.
- **Given up:** catching cleverly worded messages. The page says so.

## 8. Two clocks instead of one

- **Options:** one clock from alert to acknowledgement, or one to acknowledge and a second to act.
- **Picked:** two clocks.
- **Why:** with one clock, clicking "seen" makes the response time look perfect while nothing happens. That was the guard-rail on the demand measure.
- **Given up:** simplicity. Owners carry two deadlines.

## 9. One "AI health score"

- **Options:** combine the measures into one score, or keep them separate with separate owners.
- **Picked:** separate.
- **Why:** a single score hides which part is failing and who should act. Each measure here drives one decision for one owner.
- **Given up:** a single number for a board slide.
